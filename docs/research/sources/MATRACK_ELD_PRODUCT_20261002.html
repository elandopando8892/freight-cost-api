ELD FAQ

The questions roadside, dispatch, and finance ask.

Don't see yours? Ask compliance →

An electronic logging device or ELD is a hardware that can be connected to a vehicle's engine via OBD port to record data regarding engine and ignition status, driving time, location, and more. Its primary purpose is documenting hours of service per DOT requirements.
ELDs automatically record date, time, location information, engine hours, vehicle miles, driver identification, authorized user information, vehicle details, and motor carrier data. Some providers offer integrated systems documenting additional metrics like speed and driving violations, which optimize fleet operations though aren't mandated for DOT compliance.
ELDs function on wireless or smartphone devices meeting technical specifications set forth in the ELD rule. Devices must access the vehicle's engine to retrieve data. The FMCSA maintains a certified devices list; checking this list before purchasing ensures accuracy in documenting distance traveled.
Matrack distinguishes itself through five key aspects: complimentary FMCSA-approved device ($200 value), affordable monthly subscriptions starting at $19.95, lifetime warranty coverage, no binding contracts, and round-the-clock customer support responding within minutes.
When vehicles travel faster than 5mph, ELDs automatically designate driving status. Once speed reaches 0mph for three consecutive seconds, the system switches to non-driving status. If drivers fail confirming proper status after five minutes of stationary time, ELDs automatically reclassify to on-duty non-driving status.
Drivers operating within 150-mile radius of work reporting location may use time records instead of ELDs if released from duty for an 8-10 hour break within 12 hours and accurately document hours. Exceeding 150 miles requires paper logs that day; exceeding this eight times monthly mandates ELD usage thereafter.
